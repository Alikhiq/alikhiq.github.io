# What the code can really do (kit facts for auditors)

- Hero car: Khronos "Car Concept" GLB (CC BY 4.0), 213k triangles, 11.8 MB. A low mid-engine concept coupe, no OEM badge, full interior. Named materials: `Paint 1 Carmine`, `Paint 2 Carmine` (body paint, re-colourable live), `Glass`, `Rim1`, `Rim2`, `Tireside`, `Tiretread`, `Brake`, `Disc`, `Headlight`, `Brakelight`, `Signallight`, `Panel Sides`, interior materials. Verified: paint swaps to satin black and gloss white in three.js. See car-ref-front34.png and car-ref-side.png (plain real-time renders, no art direction yet).
- No good open-source van / box truck / food truck exists: only toy-like low-poly models (2k-7k triangles). So the commercial theme cannot show a realistic 3D van. It can show EveryWrap's real job photos (about 640-960 px each: Sprinter van, library bus, CR-V, Lexus, wake boat, food truck) as textured panels or cards.
- Rendering: three.js (WebGL2) with MeshPhysicalMaterial (clearcoat, sheen, iridescence, transmission), HDRI image-based lighting from Poly Haven (CC0), PBR textures from ambientCG (CC0), bloom, tone mapping. Clip-plane or shader-mask reveal between two materials on the same mesh. Emissive light bars / arches that move. Planar floor reflection or a blurred mirror. HTML/CSS text and controls on top of the canvas; text can also sit behind the car as a plane in the 3D scene.
- Not available: people or hands, photoreal city streets, rain simulation, cloth simulation of vinyl film, any car other than the hero model, real-time ray tracing. Motion blur is only fake (stretched emissive streaks).
- Phones: must hold a steady frame rate on a mid-range phone, so one car, limited post-processing, no heavy transparency stacks.
- Interaction available: scroll position, drag/orbit, tap, device tilt (with permission), pointer position.
